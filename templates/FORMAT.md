# Preserve the issue format

Use your own accepted issues as format references. Preserve their greeting,
section order, length, footer and reader promise while improving the explanation.
Write full, connected paragraphs with specific examples. A source or writer can
inform your editorial choices without supplying your sentences or experiences.

For this essay lane, start with 850 to 1,100 body words. An issue may reach
1,300 when its argument needs the space. State a clear claim in the title and
add a specific supporting idea in the subtitle. Name the topic in the opening.
Develop one continuous argument through roughly five to seven turning sections,
one earned study or historical example, and an inversion that tests the thesis.

Explain who participated in a study, who acted, who received the action, what
was measured and what the comparison supports. Finish with three distinct
actions about the theme and a final concrete image that completes the argument.
Those actions should each be useful on their own. Merge a heading when it breaks
the explanation, and record a material format departure in the review notes.

Count headings and the reader-facing footer as body words. Exclude title,
subtitle, source notes and production notes. Record that scope with the count.

Use a dated source window and retain topic exclusions in your private issue
state. Track creator rotation when it affects variety. A search failure is a
reason to investigate another qualifying source, not silently widen the window.
Separate source publication dates from event dates and record retrieval dates.

The disclosure must describe the actual authorship. If AI wrote the sentences,
say so and identify whether it used supplied author notes or current sources.
Do not imply the human read every source or reviewed the final draft unless
that happened. A disclosure never substitutes for factual review.
