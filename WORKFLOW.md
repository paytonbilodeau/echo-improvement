# The Echo Improvement issue workflow

## Establish the issue

Use a stable key such as your-publication:2026-10-12. Read the last published issues and any existing package for this key. Check whether the human author has claimed this week; if so, help only with the requested editing or research. Resume a partial package rather than starting a duplicate.

Choose one thesis the reader can state in a sentence. Select current, original sources that support it, and record why the thesis differs from recent issues. A source publication date is not the date of the event it describes. Read the full relevant source rather than using a headline as evidence.

For an AI teaching issue, separate released capability, previews, observed results and your inference. For a study-based essay, name who participated, what changed, what was measured and which comparison supports the claim. A vivid anecdote is not proof a result generalizes.

## Draft with a purpose

Use [the format guide](templates/FORMAT.md) with your own accepted issues. Keep the established structure while improving explanation and flow.

Develop one essay thesis through evidence and a clear causal explanation. Distinguish study participants, writers, recipients and comparison groups. Finish with three different actions about the theme, rather than three steps that all describe the same action.

Opening guidance: Name the subject immediately. Use a concrete scene or question only when it helps explain the subject.

Write full, connected sentences and paragraphs. Keep the explanation that connects an example to the thesis. Use source links where they support a claim, and retain meaningful uncertainty. Do not invent personal experiences or claim the author read or watched something without evidence.

State AI involvement truthfully. A possible disclosure is “I developed this issue with AI assistance and reviewed the sources and final draft.” Use it only if that is what happened. Do not imply a human review occurred when it did not.

## Review the article

Check the central claim against the original source, then review every other material claim. Check the headline promises what the article delivers. Read the draft as continuous prose: examples must explain the thesis, transitions must connect, and the ending must add a decision or useful action.

Use a separate critic pass with the issue brief and sources. Resolve supported objections rather than chasing a numerical score. Record the actual check in ARTICLE-REVIEW.md. A weak source pack or unsupported central claim stops the issue.

## Save the draft and covers

Use the correct publication account. Save as an unpublished draft, then reopen it and verify title, subtitle, body, links, cover and unpublished state. A successful save click is not sufficient evidence.

For a Substack workflow, put the cover in the intended social-preview/cover field. Do not add a duplicate image to the article body by accident. Prepare separate 16:9 and 5:2 exports by native adaptation or regeneration when appropriate; do not stretch or blindly crop the same image.

If saving or uploading fails, preserve the complete article, links and available assets in the private issue package. Report the failed step and deliver the copy directly for review. A failed cover upload does not erase a completed article.

## Adapt the visual story

Build a carousel only from the reviewed article. Each slide must explain or advance the idea introduced by the previous one. If you can shuffle the slides freely without changing the argument, revise the order. Preserve caveats that control the conclusion.

Use a visual style you own or can license. Require a relevant scene, readable short text and full-size and phone-size checks. For a real logo, use its official structure as the reference and inspect the result. No private style recipe is required by this template.

Prepare the ordered 4:5 image masters, a LinkedIn document when supported, and a complete four-image adaptation for X. Platform limits can change; check the route before export. A TikTok app-inbox handoff should leave sound choice and manual posting explicit, unless you separately authorize an automatic route.

## Publish only within your authority

The article and social package have separate publication gates. These templates default both to draft-only. Confirm your account, destination, exact content and authority before changing a live platform.

Once authorized, check for an existing queued or published record for this issue and destination. An uncertain submission is a reason to inspect the original job, not immediately submit another one. Wait for a terminal result and independently read the live destination.

Save the provider IDs, content and asset hashes, public URL, observed state and next action. Report draft, queued, provider-accepted, published and inbox-draft states accurately. Never call an upload or accepted job a publication. If the article is unpublished, a social CTA can invite readers to subscribe for future issues; it cannot claim the article is available.

## Learn without rewriting the evidence

After the issue, record one concrete editorial improvement and why it helped. Keep the accepted format and reader promise. Do not claim a process is improving just because a self-assigned score rose.
